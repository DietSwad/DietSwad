Jaggery vs Refined Sugar vs Desi Khand: Which Is Actually Healthier?

7 min read · 29 August 2026 · By Diet Swad

Jaggery, desi khand and refined sugar all come from the same place — sugarcane juice. What separates them is how far that juice is processed before it reaches your kitchen. Jaggery is the least processed and keeps the molasses that carries small amounts of iron, potassium and magnesium. Desi khand sits in the middle: crystallised, but never bleached or stripped bare. Refined white sugar is the most processed of the three, and what is left at the end is essentially pure sucrose.

That is the honest answer to the comparison. The less honest part of the internet is what happens next — where jaggery gets called a health food. It is not. It is a better-quality sugar, and that is a genuinely useful distinction, but it is still sugar. Here is what actually changes between the three, and what it means when you are standing in a shop reading the back of a packet.

All three start as the same thing

Sugarcane is crushed, and the juice is cleaned and boiled. Everything after that is a decision about how much of the original cane to keep and how much to remove.

Refined sugar (chini)

The juice is clarified, filtered, crystallised and spun to separate out the molasses entirely, then whitened. Removing the molasses is what removes the colour — and the trace minerals travel out with it. What you are left with is close to pure sucrose, with essentially nothing else in it. It is consistent, cheap and neutral-tasting, which is exactly why the packaged food industry runs on it.

Jaggery (gur)

The juice is boiled down and set into blocks with the molasses left in. That is the entire difference, and it is why jaggery is brown, why it tastes of caramel and minerals rather than plain sweetness, and why it carries small amounts of iron, potassium, magnesium and calcium that refined sugar does not. Its composition varies from batch to batch and region to region, because it is an agricultural product rather than a standardised industrial one.

Desi khand

Desi khand is crystallised like sugar but stops short of full refining — it is not bleached and it is not stripped of everything the cane brought with it. It keeps a pale golden colour and a soft, rounded sweetness, and it behaves in baking the way sugar does, which jaggery does not always do. That combination is the reason it exists: the texture of sugar, without the last, harshest stage of processing.

The short version: jaggery is the least processed, desi khand is partly refined, and white sugar is fully refined. The gap between them is real — but it is a gap in processing and trace minerals, not in calories, and not a reason to eat more of any of them.

So is jaggery actually healthier than sugar?

Marginally, and not for the reason most people think.

The minerals in jaggery are real, but they are present in small quantities. To get a meaningful amount of iron from jaggery alone, you would have to eat far more of it than anyone sensibly should — and by then the sugar you consumed to get there has more than cancelled the benefit. Anyone who tells you jaggery is a good source of iron is quietly skipping that part.

What jaggery genuinely gives you is a less processed ingredient with more flavour, made the way it has been made in India for generations. That is a fair reason to prefer it. "It is basically healthy, so I can have more" is not.

The same applies to desi khand. Choosing it over refined sugar is choosing a shorter, gentler process and a better-tasting ingredient — nothing more dramatic than that, and nothing less useful.

One thing worth being direct about: if you are managing diabetes or have been told to watch your blood sugar, treat all three as sugar. Jaggery and desi khand are not free passes, and this article is not medical advice — your doctor's guidance beats anything you read online, including us.

Where whole dates come in

Dates are a different category altogether, and they are worth separating out.

With jaggery, desi khand and sugar, the sweetness has been extracted from the plant and concentrated. With a whole date, the sugar is still inside the fruit, arriving alongside the fibre and the rest of the food it grew in. Nothing was pulled out. That is a genuine structural difference, not a marketing one — although it is still worth saying plainly that dates are high in sugar and a date-based snack will show a high sugar figure on the label. It is simply sugar that never left the fruit.

How to read the label yourself

You do not need to take anyone's word for this, ours included. Three habits cover most of it:

  • Read the ingredient list, not the front of the pack. Ingredients are listed in descending order by weight. If a sweetener appears first or second, the product is mostly sweetener, whatever the front says.
  • Check whether the sweetener is even named. "Natural sweetener" is not an ingredient. Jaggery, desi khand, dates — those are ingredients. A brand that is proud of what it uses will name it, and increasingly will tell you the percentage too.
  • Look past the sweetener. Jaggery on the front of a pack means very little if the same list also contains refined flour, palm oil and a row of chemical names. The sweetener is one line out of many.

Frequently asked questions

Is jaggery better than refined sugar?

It is less processed and retains small amounts of iron, potassium and magnesium that refined sugar has had removed. But it is comparable in calories and is still sugar, so it should be treated as sugar in your daily intake — it is a better-quality choice, not a health food.

What is desi khand?

Desi khand is partially refined cane sugar. It is crystallised like white sugar but is not bleached or fully stripped of molasses, so it keeps a golden colour and a softer flavour while still behaving like sugar in baking.

Is desi khand the same as jaggery?

No. Jaggery is boiled-down cane juice set into blocks with the molasses left in, so it is darker and stronger-tasting. Desi khand is crystallised and paler, sitting between jaggery and refined sugar in how far it has been processed.

Are date-sweetened snacks low in sugar?

Not necessarily, and usually not. Dates are naturally high in sugar, so a date-based snack will often show a high figure on the label. The difference is that the sugar is still contained within the whole fruit along with its fibre, rather than having been extracted and concentrated.

The honest conclusion

Jaggery is the least processed. Desi khand is partly refined. White sugar is fully refined and gives you nothing but sweetness. Choosing the first two over the third is a small, real improvement — and it is the reason we make the choices we do.

But the bigger question is never which sweetener is in your snack. It is what the other ten ingredients on the list are. That is where the real difference between a clean snack and a dressed-up one shows up, and it is the one part of the label most people never turn over to read.
